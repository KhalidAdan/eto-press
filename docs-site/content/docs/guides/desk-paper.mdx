---
title: Run a desk paper
description: A newsletter and a web presence with no feeds, no models, and no accounts you don't choose.
---

<Callout>
  The desk engine ships with **generation 2** —
  `@eto-press/engine-desk`, on npm since `2.20260816.2`.
</Callout>

A desk paper is the smallest honest publication: you write markdown, the
press prints it into the same append-only archive, the same site, the same
morning email — with the same constitution (no tracking, the brief ends) —
and *nothing else happens*. No feeds are fetched, no models run, no GPU is
needed.

## Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Declare the engine">
    In your paper's `eto.toml`:

    ```toml
    [paper]
    name = "the desk paper"
    motto = "The editor writes. The press prints."

    [engine]
    use = "desk"
    ```

    A desk paper needs no `[[source]]` blocks at all.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write onto the desk">
    ```text
    desk/2026-08-16-on-quiet-days.md
    ```

    ```markdown
    # On quiet days

    Some mornings there is nothing to add to what the world already said.
    ```

    The first `# heading` becomes the headline. No heading, and the
    filename — de-dated, de-dashed — serves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Print">
    ```bash
    eto press
    ```
    Every entry not yet printed becomes a story in this morning's edition.
    Each entry prints exactly once — the press journals a content hash — so
    editing an already-printed file makes it print again as a new entry,
    which is exactly what a revised thought deserves.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The empty desk

A morning with nothing new prints nothing at all: no archive file, no email,
one honest line in the log — *the desk holds nothing new. The press rests.*
Your readers get email only when you wrote something, which is most of the
reason they will stay subscribed.

## What carries over from the news engine

Everything that is the platform's rather than the doctrine's: the archive
that never rewrites, [corrections](/docs/guides/corrections) that point
back, the [tier ladder](/docs/tiers), the
[morning email](/docs/guides/mail) with its double-opt-in list you own, and
[skins](/docs/concepts/skins) over the same semantic anatomy — a desk story
is simply a story with no differ block and no sources line, and the default
theme sets it accordingly.

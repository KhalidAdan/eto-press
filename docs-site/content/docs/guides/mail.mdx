A paper with readers sends one email a day, at most. It ends, like the brief
ends: no tracking pixels, no click wrapping, an unsubscribe link in every
footer that actually works. On a morning with no edition, nothing is sent.

## Provision SES

Amazon SES is the recommended provider for a real mailing list — roughly the
cost of a coffee per year at household scale.

<Callout>
  One-command provisioning is **roadmap phase 3**; there is no mail-setup
  verb in the CLI yet. Today the steps below are performed once, by hand, in
  the AWS console. The press automates everything after provisioning: the
  sends, the guards, the suppression, the backups.
</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identity and DKIM">
    Create the domain identity in SES and add the DKIM records it gives you
    at your DNS host. Verification usually lands within the hour.
  </Step>
  <Step title="List and configuration set">
    Create a contact list with one topic, and a configuration set. Their
    names go in `[mail]` below; the defaults are `eto-readers`,
    `morning-edition`, and `eto-mail`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secrets">
    Create an IAM user allowed to send through SES and manage the list. Its
    keys go in your paper's `.env` (never committed; `.env.example` shows
    the shape). `eto doctor` then checks the account: sending enabled, and
    the daily quota. It does not check the domain identity or the list —
    those you confirm in the console.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn it on">
    ```toml
    email_edition = true
    ```
    in `sources.toml`. Until the domain verifies, sends go from
    `[mail] from_fallback` — a verified address, with a note in the log so
    you notice. If `from_fallback` is empty too, the send exits 1 with
    *no mail identity configured* rather than send as nobody.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The mail block

```toml
[mail]
region = "ca-central-1"
domain = "example.news"
from = "example <brief@example.news>"
from_fallback = "you@example.com"   # verified; used until the domain is
contact_list = "example-readers"
topic = "morning-edition"
config_set = "eto-mail"
```

Full field reference: [configuration](/docs/reference/configuration#mail).

## The guards

- **Idempotent sends** — a retried morning never mails twice; the send is
  guarded per edition.
- **Suppression swept** before every send; bounces and complaints leave your
  list automatically.
- **Quota pacing** respects your provider's send rate.
- **Test first**: `eto email --test you@example.com` sends the latest
  published edition to one address and nobody else. `--test success`,
  `--test bounce`, and `--test complaint` are shorthands for the SES mailbox
  simulator — the whole path, including the suppression sweep, without a
  real inbox or a dent in your reputation.

## The subscribe flow

The subscribe flow is a small pair of edge functions: a form endpoint with a
honeypot, and a confirm link — double opt-in, so your list is consent all
the way down. No verb deploys them; you do, once:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy the functions into the paper">
    Copy the `functions/` directory from the `@eto-press/subscribe` package
    into the paper, beside `site/`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate their configuration">
    ```bash
    eto gen-functions
    ```
    writes `functions/_config.ts` from `eto.toml`. Rerun it whenever your
    masthead changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy">
    ```bash
    wrangler pages deploy site
    ```
    Then set three bindings on the Pages project: `SUBSCRIBE_SECRET`,
    `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The list is yours: exportable any time (`eto backup-readers`), never
enriched with anything you did not ask for.

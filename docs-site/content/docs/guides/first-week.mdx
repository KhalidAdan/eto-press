---
title: Your first week
description: How to read the brief, when to check a source, and what the report is telling you.
---

The press succeeds by being finished — present for a few minutes, trusted
because it was checkable, and then done for the day. The first week is where
that trust gets earned or doesn't.

## Day one: read one edition, whole

Six minutes, maybe eight. Notice the four parts of each story: what happened,
**where the accounts differ**, the sources line, and the end. The differ
block is the part that exists nowhere else — it is the paper telling you, in
plain words, where the outlets you chose part company.

## Sometime this week: check a source

A line will seem too clean — about a hospital, a budget, a resignation.
Click the outlet's name in the sources line. It links to the account the
press actually read, not a homepage. Find the claim. It should say what the
brief said it said, because the verification cage checked every quote and
entity against that text before the story shipped.

Do this four or five times over the coming weeks. When it checks out each
time, you will mostly stop — and the stopping will be earned rather than
lazy. If it ever does *not* check out, that is a defect in the press, and it
is reported and fixed like any other defect.

## Read the run report like an editor

Every edition ends with the morning's instrument panel. Three lines matter
most in week one:

- **Feeds read: 44 of 46; failed: …** — an outlet that keeps failing is
  either having a bad week or closing its front door. The press retries
  transient failures and reports the rest; a persistent refuser is a
  candidate for [replacement](/docs/guides/editing-sources).
- **Balance notes** — *no source labeled lean-right covered this story* is a
  measurement of your masthead, printed under the story it measures. A
  drumbeat of the same note is your source list telling you where it is
  thin.
- **Story #N dropped: …** — every drop has a named reason: too few fetched
  accounts, a draft that failed verification. Drops are the press choosing a
  gap over a guess, in public.

## The below-the-fold experiment

One story per edition arrives from outside the front page: the model's single
nomination, chosen because its consequence exceeds its coverage, with its
reason printed verbatim. You are the grader. If the nominations bore you,
kill the feature with one line in `sources.toml`:

```toml
below_the_fold = false
```

## The end of the week

You have now seen a correction run (or you will soon): dated, at the top of a
later edition, pointing back at the original, which stands unchanged. Being
able to watch a story be wrong and then be corrected is most of what it means
to trust a paper.

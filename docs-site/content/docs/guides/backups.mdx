---
title: Backups and the journal
description: What must survive, what can be rebuilt, and the two commands that cover both.
---

Not everything in a paper is equally precious, and the backup story follows
that honestly.

## What can never be rebuilt

- **The archive** (`archive/*.md`) — the record itself. It is plain files;
  version it, sync it, copy it. The flagship keeps it in git.
- **Four journal tables** that came from you and your mornings rather than
  from the world: your corrections, the source-health history
  (`feed_fetches`), the published-edition store (`published_stories`), and
  the record of what was mailed (`email_sends`).
- **Your configuration** — `eto.toml`, `sources.toml`, `models.lock.json`,
  and (tier 3) the reader list.

## What can

Everything else in the journal — items, verdicts, drafts, verifications —
is a cache of work the press can redo from the world. Losing it costs one
slow morning, not history.

## The two commands

```bash
eto export
```

exports five tables — `stories`, `feed_fetches`, `email_sends`, `clusters`,
`corrections` — as diffable JSONL into `db/exports/`: text files that
belong in version control beside the archive, where a bad day is a readable
diff instead of a binary mystery.

```bash
eto backup
```

takes a rotating snapshot of the whole journal (SQLite online backup — safe
while the press runs) to your configured backup directory, keeping the last
N:

```toml
[backup]
dir = "D:\\paper-backups"   # point this at a different disk
keep = 14
```

The default `dir` is `backups`, a gitignored folder inside the paper — a
snapshot on the same disk as the thing it protects. Point it elsewhere when
you configure the paper for real.

On tier 3, `eto backup-readers` snapshots the reader list from SES, so your
list survives even your mail provider. It runs at the tail of every
`eto press` whatever the tier; without AWS credentials it fails and the
morning carries on.

All three run automatically at the tail of every `eto press` morning; the
commands exist for the days you want one now.

## Restoring

- **Archive**: it's files — put them back.
- **Journal**: copy a snapshot over `db/eto.sqlite`, or delete the journal
  and let the next morning rebuild the caches. Corrections and history
  then come back only from the exports, and only by hand: there is no
  import verb, so load the JSONL rows with `sqlite3` or a short script.
- **A whole paper on a new machine**: config + archive + the latest journal
  snapshot, then `eto doctor` — it will tell you what's missing (usually
  just the models). `eto models pull` downloads them; if the registry's
  current build differs from your lock, the press reports drift and
  `eto models pin` accepts it.

---
title: Editing your sources
description: Dropping an outlet, adding one, and reading the measurements that tell you when.
---

The source list is the whole reason this is *your* paper. Expect to edit it
about twice a year — and to understand exactly what you changed each time.

## When to drop an outlet

Two honest reasons, both visible in your editions:

- **It keeps being alone on its own facts.** Stories where one outlet's
  account can't be corroborated don't run (a single-source item is a rumour
  with good manners) — so an outlet that is chronically alone contributes
  chronically nothing, while still costing fetches.
- **It keeps refusing the front door.** The run report's feed-health line
  (`Newsmax 0/7 ok`) is the measurement. eto does not disguise itself and
  does not pick locks: a source that must be tricked into being read is a
  source eto drops, and the slot is filled from elsewhere on the same side
  of the aisle. The spectrum has depth; no single outlet is owed a place in
  it.

Delete its `[[source]]` block. Done. Tomorrow's paper reflects it.

## When to add one

The balance notes tell you. If *no source labeled right covered this story*
keeps appearing, your masthead is thin on that side, and that gap is yours to
close — or to keep, knowingly. Add the outlet's front-door feed:

```toml
[[source]]
name = "National Review"
side = "right"
feeds = ["https://www.nationalreview.com/feed/"]
```

The audited catalog of every AllSides-charted outlet's front door ships with
the press (`docs/SOURCES.md` in the repository) — most additions are a
copy-paste from there.

## Side labels are yours too

The five seeded labels — `left`, `lean-left`, `center`, `lean-right`,
`right` — come from the AllSides chart, which rates perspective, not
accuracy. The chart is a cartographer, not an editor: relabel any outlet you
believe the chart has wrong, and the balance measurements will follow *your*
map. Custom labels beyond the five are allowed; they render in the spectrum
and count in balance like any other side.

## What you cannot configure, on purpose

The journalistic standards — the two-outlet minimum, the density gates, the
word budget, the verification cage — are not knobs. They are the press's
half of the bargain, the doctrine in executable form. The masthead is yours;
the standards are the press's.

## After editing

```bash
eto doctor
```

confirms every feed answers before the morning does. A flag placed below a
`[[source]]` block belongs to that source in TOML — the press refuses the
common mistake by name and tells you where the flag goes.

---
title: Running a correction
description: The archive never changes. The correction runs forward.
---

When a story turns out to have been wrong, the press does what a good paper
does: it corrects itself in public, in the next edition, without touching the
record.

## Record the correction

```bash
eto correct 2026-08-10 2 "The recall figure was 1.6 million dozen, not 1.6 million; the source has issued a correction."
```

The arguments are the edition date, the story's rank in that edition, and
your note. The correction is journaled and waits for the next morning.

## What prints

At the top of the next edition — in the archive markdown, on the site, in
the email, and in the RSS item:

> **Corrections** — In the edition of Monday, August 10, 2026, the story
> "Massive egg recall in US south and south-west": The recall figure was
> 1.6 million dozen, not 1.6 million; the source has issued a correction.
> The original stands unchanged in the archive.

The link points back at the original edition, which is exactly as wrong as
it was — that is the point. Being able to watch a story be wrong, and then
be corrected, is most of what it means to trust a paper. A press that
reached into yesterday's file and fixed it while you slept would be asking
you to trust its memory instead of its record.

## When the source was wrong, not the press

Most corrections are this kind — an outlet revised its own figure after your
edition printed. The correction note is yours to phrase; say whose error it
was. The verification cage guarantees the story faithfully reflected the
accounts *as fetched that morning*; it cannot guarantee the world stopped
moving afterward.

## Corrections are journaled forever

The corrections table is one of the two whose contents cannot be rebuilt
from the world (the other is your source-health history). It is exported as
diffable JSONL on every run and included in every backup — see
[Backups and the journal](/docs/guides/backups).

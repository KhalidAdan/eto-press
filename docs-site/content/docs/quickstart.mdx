This page takes you from nothing to a printed edition. If you want the full
requirements first, see [Installation](/docs/installation).

## Print your first edition

<Steps>
  <Step title="Initialize a paper">
    ```bash
    npx @eto-press/cli init
    ```
    The wizard asks for your paper's name, seeds `sources.toml` from the
    audited AllSides shelf, pulls and pins the models
    (`models.lock.json` is generated, never hand-edited), and runs a
    sandboxed dry edition so your first real morning is not your first test.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the press">
    ```bash
    eto doctor
    ```
    Preflight as a command: Ollama reachable, models matching the lock, VRAM
    sanity, feeds answering, the last edition's status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Print a morning">
    ```bash
    eto press
    ```
    The whole morning as one verb — print the edition, render the site,
    export the journal, send the email (if configured), take the backups.
    Expect twenty to forty minutes on the first run while the judge works
    through the morning's pairs; reruns resume from the journal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it a habit">
    ```bash
    eto schedule --time 05:30
    ```
    Installs the morning into your operating system's scheduler — Task
    Scheduler on Windows, launchd on macOS, cron or systemd on Linux.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Your edition is now three things at once: a markdown file in `archive/`
(the permanent record), a web page in `site/`, and — if you climb the
[tier ladder](/docs/tiers) — an email in your readers' inboxes.

## What just happened

The wizard created a **paper**: an ordinary directory holding your masthead
(`eto.toml`), your editorial line (`sources.toml`), your journal (`db/`), and
your archive. The press visits it each morning, prints, and leaves.
Everything about that directory is yours — see
[What is a paper?](/docs/concepts/paper)

While it printed, the press fetched every feed through the front door with an
honest user-agent, asked a small local model which items describe the same
event, clustered the matches, selected the broadest stories, fetched the full
accounts, composited each story from outlets that disagree, and then checked
every quote, entity, and adjective against the fetched text before letting it
ship. The whole walk is in [The pipeline](/docs/concepts/pipeline).

<Callout>

  `gen2-package` line and shipping with the 2.x releases. Generation-1
  papers are eto-engine papers, and stay valid forever.
</Callout>

An **engine** is the editorial half of the press: it decides what a story is,
how it must be told, and what a morning with nothing to say should do. The
[platform](/docs/concepts/press) hands an engine the morning — the date and
your masthead, nothing else — and the engine hands back an edition, or
silence.

A paper declares its engine in `eto.toml`; absent means eto:

```toml
[engine]
use = "desk"
```

## The eto engine

The flagship doctrine, and the reason the press exists:

1. **One story, many mouths.** A story is an event and the competing accounts
   of it, collapsed into one telling. A single-source item is a rumour with
   good manners, and it does not run.
2. **The disagreement is the story.** Where accounts conflict, the story says
   so, in the body, with each side named. Consensus manufactured by deleting
   the contradiction is a quieter kind of lying.
3. **Nothing unattributed ships.** The sources line is the proof of work.
4. **The model composites. It does not comment.** No adjective it was not
   given, no motive, no forecast — and a deterministic verification cage
   that checks every quote, entity, and adjective against the fetched
   accounts.
5. **Incomplete beats wrong.** Given a gap or a guess, eto prints the gap.
6. **The masthead is yours.** eto enforces the balance you configured and no
   other, and tells you plainly when your sources collapse onto one side.

It requires a source list (an eto paper without outlets is refused by name),
needs two local models — a small judge and an 8B-class compositor, both
digest-pinned — and its whole morning is
[the twelve-stage pipeline](/docs/concepts/pipeline).

## The desk engine

The null machinery: **the editor writes, the press prints.** Drop markdown
files onto the desk —

```text
desk/2026-08-16-on-quiet-days.md
```

— and each entry prints once, as a story, in the next morning's edition. The
first `# heading` line becomes the headline; no heading, and the filename
serves. A morning with nothing new prints **nothing at all** — no archive
file, no email, an honest note in the log: *the desk holds nothing new. The
press rests.*

No feeds, no judging, no models: a desk paper never needs Ollama, needs no
source list, and everything it inherits — the append-only archive, the
untracked readers, the finite brief, the mail — comes from the platform's
constitution. If you have ever wanted a newsletter and a web presence without
renting your audience from a platform that tracks them, this is the smallest
honest way to have one. Walkthrough:
[Run a desk paper](/docs/guides/desk-paper).

## Silence versus the quiet page

Engines choose what an empty morning means. The eto engine prints the quiet
page — *nothing to print today: no event was covered by two or more of your
sources* — because for a news brief, "nothing qualified" is itself news. The
desk engine chooses silence, because an empty desk is not an edition. Both
are honest; the difference is doctrine.

## The ladder

More engines are planned, each deliberately small, each forcing the next
piece of the platform into shape: an event-driven letter that prints only
when its institution speaks, a link digest, a sports paper with columns and
tables. The engine interface stays private until the ladder has enough rungs
that it can be extracted from evidence rather than guessed.

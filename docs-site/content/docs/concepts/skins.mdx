# Skins

Every page the press renders carries only **semantic anatomy** — class names
that say what things are, never how they look:

```html
<article class="story">
  <h2 class="story__headline">…</h2>
  <p class="story__body prose">…</p>
  <h3 class="story__differ-label instrument accent">Where the accounts differ</h3>
  <p class="story__differ prose">…</p>
  <p class="story__sources instrument">Sources …</p>
</article>
```

Three voice classes are shared by design, because they *are* the design:
`prose` (the news speaking), `instrument` (the paper speaking), and `accent`
(the single color, spent only on the paper's own measurements — the differ
label, the balance notes, the full stop after the wordmark).

## The default theme

The press ships one stylesheet — Lora and claret, the hairlines, a 68ch
measure — so the zero-effort path is beautiful. It is just CSS over the
anatomy above.

## Your skin

Replace the stylesheet. That is the entire mechanism: no fork, no template
override, no permission. Your paper keeps the engine's structure — the differ
block, the sources line, the end marker are guaranteed by the engine, present
and checkable in every page — and you have full editorial CSS rights over how
that structure looks.

A skin that styles its attribution invisible has not defeated the engine; it
has stopped being an eto-engine paper. Enforcement is checkability, not locks
— that norm is stated in public, by name.

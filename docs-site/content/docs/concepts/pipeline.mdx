---
title: The pipeline
description: Twelve stages from raw feeds to a verified edition, resumable at every step.
---

Every morning is the same walk. Two principles shape all of it:

1. **The database is the journal.** Every expensive unit of work — a feed
   fetch, a model verdict, a composite draft — is keyed and cached in SQLite.
   A crash at minute 28 costs the unit in flight and nothing else; recovery is
   always the same command, run again. Resume is not a feature; it is the
   absence of one.
2. **The model only touches prose.** Two stages ask a model anything: judging
   pairs and compositing stories. Every other stage is deterministic code, and
   the model's output is always caged by deterministic verification.

## The stages

<Steps>
  <Step title="Preflight">
    Validate the masthead, run migrations, confirm the engine's models are
    present and match `models.lock.json`. A drifted digest stops the press —
    an `ollama pull` must never silently change your paper's mind.
    Configuration problems fail loudly here, before any work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fetch feeds">
    Every feed of every source, through the front door, with an honest
    user-agent. Every attempt is recorded — success or not — which is where
    your source-health history comes from. A failed feed never kills the run;
    the outlet is simply absent and the report says so.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Normalize and classify">
    Parse RSS and Atom (hostile encodings assumed), then classify each item —
    news, opinion, video, podcast, liveblog, digest. Only `news` items may
    participate in event matching: opinion and format items act as glue
    between unrelated clusters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Window and prefilter">
    Keep the 48-hour window, then cut candidate pairs with plain code — two
    items must share capitalized tokens before any model sees them. This is
    a ~97% cut before a single model call. A funnel wildly off profile
    aborts the run: cheap sanity beats expensive garbage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Judge pairs">
    The small model answers one question per pair: same news event, yes or
    no. Verdicts are cached by pair, model, and prompt hash — rerunning
    skips every judged pair; changing the prompt re-judges automatically. A
    unanimous verdict stream trips an abort: one-sided answers mean a broken
    prompt, not a quiet news day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cluster, gate, dedupe">
    Union-find over the yes-edges, then a density gate that cuts the welded
    blobs transitive chaining creates. Clusters mostly published in an
    earlier edition are set aside — yesterday's front page does not reprint
    itself. A story that returns with mostly new reporting runs again as a
    development.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select">
    Deterministic eligibility: two or more distinct outlets, ranked by
    breadth of outlets and sides, capped — the brief ends by design. A
    one-sided story runs, carrying its measurement in print: *no source
    labeled lean-right covered this story.*
  </Step>
  <Step title="Nominate below the fold">
    One model pick from outside the front page: the story whose consequence
    most exceeds its coverage. Its reason is printed verbatim for you to
    judge, it can never displace a main story, and `below_the_fold = false`
    kills the feature entirely.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fetch the accounts">
    The full article behind every selected item — front door, politeness
    delay. An account that cannot be fetched drops out of the composite: the
    sources line names only accounts actually read. A story below two
    fetched accounts is dropped and reported.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Composite">
    The larger model merges the fetched accounts into the four-part story:
    headline, body, where-the-accounts-differ, sources. No adjective it was
    not given, no motive, no forecast, ~350 words, it ends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    The cage. Deterministic checks against the fetched text: every quote
    verbatim, every entity present, no evaluative adjective in the paper's
    own voice, the sources line exact, the word budget respected. Violations
    go back to the compositor once as editor's notes; a story that still
    fails is cut or dropped. A gap over a guess, every time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Render, archive, report">
    Surviving stories become the edition — markdown in the archive (written
    once, atomically, never overwritten), the site, the email, the RSS item.
    The run report prints the whole morning as data: feeds read and failed,
    the funnel, every drop and its named reason, source-health trends.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When something fails

Every failure is a named error with structured fields, and one rule decides
its blast radius: **configuration and distribution problems stop the press
loudly; unit-of-work problems degrade the brief quietly and honestly.** A
missing outlet is a smaller brief. A broken prompt is a stopped press. The
full catalog is in [the error reference](/docs/reference/errors).

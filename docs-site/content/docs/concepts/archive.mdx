---
title: The archive and the report
description: Being able to watch a story be wrong, and then be corrected, is most of what trust is.
---


## The archive is fixed

Yesterday's brief is the record of what was known yesterday, and it stays that
way — one markdown file per edition, written once, atomically, never rewritten.
When a story turns out to have been wrong, the correction runs in **today's**
brief, dated, pointing back:

```bash
npm run correct -- 2026-08-10 2 "The figure was wrong; the source has issued a correction."
```

The press never reaches into what it has already told you and changes it while
you sleep. Nobody's press should.

## The run, reported

Every edition ends with its own instrument panel, printed as data, not advice:
which feeds answered and which refused, the funnel from raw items to published
stories, every story dropped and the named reason it dropped, and the health
trends of your sources over the past week.

This is where "your sources have collapsed onto one side" appears — a
measurement you are entitled to. The press will tell you plainly; it will not
quietly correct you. An editor who can be overruled by his own press is not an
editor.

## When something breaks

Every failure in the pipeline is a named error with structured fields, and the
row-level rule is constant: **configuration problems stop the press loudly;
unit-of-work problems degrade the brief quietly and honestly.** A missing
outlet is a smaller brief. A broken prompt is a stopped press. The 6 a.m.
debugging session starts in the run report, never in a stack trace.

# Sports — a desk note

*A desk note, not a constitution. The brief's constitution can move a
vote; this desk can be changed by its editor without ceremony, and this
note says which parts. Drafted 2026-09-26. The section is not yet
printing: it arrives with press generation 3 and the ledger engine.*

## What it is

Last night's games and the writers worth reading about them. Three
things, in this order:

1. **The scores.** A board read from the leagues' own doors: results and,
   in a playoff, the state of the series. Figures with motion, each
   naming its door. A scoreboard served to a league's own readers
   without a key is a front door.
2. **The columns.** The editor's own signed pieces, left on the desk.
   A byline always means a human.
3. **The writers.** Decked links from named writers' feeds: trade talk,
   rumours, the long read on a bad bench. Grouped by the shelf the
   source file puts them on. The writer's own blurb is printed when the
   feed carries one; the press writes a deck only when it does not.

The story of how the playoffs are going comes from the writers, never
from the press. Nothing is composited.

## What it is not

Not a results service, not a live blog, not every game. The source file
names the leagues that matter to this paper and the writers it trusts,
and the section is exactly that wide.

## What may change without ceremony

Which leagues, which doors, which writers, how many links, whether the
columns lead or follow. The source file is the desk.

## What may not

Attribution: every score names its door, every link is the writer's own.
A byline is never invented. The deck cage and the front door, as on
every desk.

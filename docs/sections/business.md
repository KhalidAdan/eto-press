# Business — a desk note

*A desk note, not a constitution. The brief's constitution can move a
vote; this desk can be changed by its editor without ceremony, and this
note says which parts. Drafted 2026-09-26. The section is not yet
printing: it arrives with press generation 3 and the ledger engine.*

## What it is

The lay of the land as of yesterday. Two things, in this order:

1. **The board.** A short table of figures with their motion since the
   last edition: indices, rates, whatever doors the source file names.
   Each figure names the door it was read from.
2. **The desk.** Decked links from business outlets: a headline, one
   sentence that says what the piece says, the outlet, and the link. The
   outlet's own blurb is printed when it wrote one; the press writes a
   deck only when the feed carried none.

Nothing is composited. No model writes prose here beyond a deck, and a
deck adds nothing the piece did not say.

## What it is not

Not a market call, not a forecast, not a narrative of the week. The
board shows what moved; the links show who wrote about it. Which of the
two matters is the reader's.

## What may change without ceremony

Which boards, which doors, which outlets, how many links, the order of
the boards. The source file is the desk; edit it and the desk changes.

## What may not

Attribution: every figure names its door, every link is the outlet's own
front door. The deck cage: a deck that says a number, a name, or a
quoted phrase the piece does not contain is refused. The front door:
a door that must be tricked into being read is dropped, not picked.

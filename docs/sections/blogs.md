# Blogs — a desk note

*A desk note, not a constitution. The brief's constitution can move a
vote; this desk can be changed by its editor without ceremony, and this
note says which parts. Drafted 2026-09-26. The section is not yet
printing: it arrives with press generation 3 and the shelf engine.*

## What it is

The writers this paper follows, one line each. Every new post since the
last edition prints as a numbered row: the title as the link, one
sentence that says what the post says, the writer's domain. No images,
no bodies, no retelling. Rows are grouped by the shelf the source file
puts a writer on.

A blog post is single-source by nature and that is fine here: the row
is a pointer, not a story, and the click is the whole point.

## The deck

The one sentence under each title is a **deck**, in the newsroom sense:
written by the desk, not the author, and its whole discipline is that it
adds nothing the post did not say. When the writer's feed carries its
own summary, that summary is the deck and the press writes nothing.
Only a post whose feed carried the full body, or nothing at all, gets a
deck from the model, and the model's deck is refused if it is longer
than two sentences or contains a number, a name, or a quoted phrase the
post does not.

## What it is not

Not a feed reader. It ends. Not a ranking. Rows are in the order the
source file names the shelves, then newest first. Not a summary of the
blogosphere. It is exactly the writers in the file.

## What may change without ceremony

Which writers, which shelves, how many rows per shelf, whether the
writer's blurb or the model's deck is preferred when both exist (today:
the writer's).

## What may not

Every row links the post. The deck cage. The front door.
